# Sleeve Notes: richer links between records

Every record has more context than its title and artist. Sometimes that context
can give a DJ a natural way into a link: the album it came from, a trusted
release year, or the fact that it is returning to the station after a long
absence.

Sleeve Notes give the DJ a small amount of that useful context without turning
every break into a mini biography. They are optional, verified details that a
presenter can use when they genuinely help—or leave alone when the record needs
nothing more than a well-judged introduction.

## What listeners will notice

A good Sleeve Note should feel like a DJ noticing something worth sharing, not
like a database being read aloud. One link might mention a release year; the
next might simply name the artist and let the presenter react to the record.

For example, if SUB/WAVE knows that **Mira Vale — “Glass Lines”** was released
in **2018**, a DJ might say:

> “Mira Vale with *Glass Lines*. There’s a lovely glow to this one—released in
> 2018, and settling in beautifully.”

The release year is supplied by the station. “Lovely glow” is the presenter’s
own response. That distinction keeps the link human: facts give it something
real to stand on, while the DJ still decides what is interesting and how to say
it.

## Small, useful, and optional

Sleeve Notes are intentionally sparse. For any one link, SUB/WAVE offers the
DJ no more than two supplemental details, selected in a stable order from
information the station already knows. Depending on the record and its history
at your station, those may include:

- the album;
- a trusted release year;
- that it is the station's first play; or
- a rare return that has only been heard once or twice before, after a
  meaningful gap.

Not every track has useful notes, and no note is compulsory. The DJ may use a
single detail, rephrase it naturally, or say nothing about it at all. That
restraint is deliberate: a track link should serve the record and the moment,
not prove how much information SUB/WAVE can retrieve.

## Facts and reaction have different jobs

Sleeve Notes are part of the factual boundary for DJ links. The DJ can express
an opinion, make a connection, or sound delighted, wry, hushed or playful in
character. What it cannot do is expand a short fact into a larger piece of
music history from its own memory.

An album title does not establish a story about that album. A first station
play is not a premiere. A release year is not permission to infer an era,
influence, chart position, collaborator, credit, or artist biography.

This may sound strict, but it gives the presenter a clearer job. Rather than
trying to fill every gap with plausible detail, the DJ can stay present with
the music and say only what it can stand behind.

## What you can control

The **Release-year mentions** control in **Settings → DJ behaviour → Link
style** decides how often a trusted year is made available for an eligible
link:

- **Regular** keeps release years available whenever they are known.
- **Occasional** makes them available for roughly one in four eligible links.
- **Rare** makes them available for roughly one in six eligible links.

This is a variety control, not a switch for whether a year is trustworthy. The
year remains verified in the library; the setting simply helps prevent it from
becoming the DJ's favourite piece of connective tissue.

## What Sleeve Notes are not

Sleeve Notes do not search the web, scrape music sites, or promise a fuller
story for every artist and record. They use information SUB/WAVE already has
and work perfectly well when that information is limited.

They are also different from **Extended Sleeve Notes**, a future optional
feature for source-backed editorial context. That feature is not required for
the factual boundary or the everyday, station-known details described here.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to write Sleeve Notes myself?

No. The current Sleeve Notes are assembled automatically from trusted library
and station-history information. Your role is still important: good metadata,
a clear DJ brief, and sensible house rules make the available context more
useful without requiring you to script individual links.

### Will every track get a fact-filled introduction?

No. A link receives only a small selection when suitable details are available,
and the DJ is free not to use them. Many of the best links will remain short.

### Can the DJ add other facts it knows?

No. For music-history claims, the DJ works from the supplied facts. It may give
an in-character reaction to the record, but it should not supplement a Sleeve
Note with remembered or plausible-sounding trivia.

### Does this replace my show brief or DJ Soul?

No. Sleeve Notes provide the reliable material. Your show brief, house rules
and DJ Soul provide the perspective, voice and judgement that make the link
feel like it belongs on your station.
