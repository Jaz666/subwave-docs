# Learning from the Booth Log

The Booth Log is where a station becomes easier to tune. It lets you look back
at what a DJ chose, what informed the choice and what was said on air, rather
than trying to diagnose the whole station from one surprising transition.

Treat it as a listening notebook, not a performance scoreboard. A single odd
pick can be a perfectly good bit of variety; a pattern over several shows is
useful evidence.

## Two different kinds of explanation

A **Shortlist Pick** can include a private selection reason. It records why the
DJ model preferred that record from the candidates it was given. That is
editorial working, not a script for the microphone.

The on-air link has a different job: it should sound natural, stay within the
facts supplied for that moment and leave room for the music. It does not need
to explain the selection process to the listener. A thoughtful private reason
and a short, unforced link are therefore a healthy pairing, not a mismatch.

For example, Lucy's choice of Morcheeba — *Blue Chair* after The Orb could be
explained privately through its calm morning energy and her preference for warm,
melodic music. On air, the DJ does not need to recite that reasoning. See
[Giving your DJ Musical Leanings](giving-your-dj-musical-leanings.md) for the
full example.

## Review a small run, not one moment

Choose a show and read or listen to six to twelve turns from different parts of
it. Include ordinary links, a few track choices and any handoffs or unusual
moments. Then ask:

- Does this feel like the same show throughout, without every link sounding the
  same?
- Are the choices broad enough for the show's promise, but still recognisable
  as its musical world?
- Do the private selection reasons make sensible use of the current track,
  show context and Musical Leanings?
- Are the spoken links concise and grounded in the supplied context?
- Is one repeated habit appearing: the same kind of opener, the same safe
  artist, forced observations, or a DJ who talks when the programme needs air?

Write down the pattern in plain language before changing anything. “The late
show's picks are all familiar favourites” is actionable. “The DJ is bad at
picking” is not.

## Change the layer that owns the problem

Different parts of SUB/WAVE do different jobs. The Booth Log is most helpful
when it stops you fixing a programming issue with a personality rewrite, or a
prompt issue with a longer playlist.

| What you keep seeing | First place to review |
| --- | --- |
| Every DJ sounds like the same presenter | DJ Souls; then the shared system prompt if the issue is station-wide |
| A show has no sense of its time, purpose or pace | Show brief and show settings |
| Choices are too safe, too obvious or too similar | Musical Leanings, filters and shortlist pass count |
| A track is outside the intended musical territory | Show settings, playlists and filters |
| Links repeatedly invent atmosphere or force trivia | System prompt and station house rules |
| Errors, fallbacks or failed picks appear | Check the underlying setup before changing creative guidance |

Make one small change, then give it enough turns to show its effect. Rewriting a
Soul, show brief and Leanings at the same time may improve the result, but it
makes it impossible to know which change mattered.

## What good variation looks like

There is no single correct successor to a track. The same current record can
produce different credible shortlists on different runs, especially when a
station deliberately leaves room for discovery. That is not drift by itself.

Look instead for whether the reasons remain connected to the programme's
identity. A DJ with a leaning towards overlooked, guitar-led records should not
always choose an obscure track, but its close calls should occasionally resolve
in that direction. If every decision resolves towards the most familiar option,
that is a useful signal to revisit the show's preferences.

## A simple review routine

Once a week, or after a meaningful change:

1. Pick one show and one listener moment: breakfast, the working day, a late
   programme, or a specialist slot.
2. Read a short run in the Booth Log and listen to a few actual links.
3. Name one repeatable strength and one repeatable friction.
4. Adjust the one setting layer most likely to own that friction.
5. Check another short run later in the week.

This keeps the station responsive without turning it into a constant prompt
experiment. The aim is not to eliminate surprise; it is to make sure the
surprises still feel like they belong.

## Common questions

### Should I judge every track choice from its reason?

No. Reasons are most valuable as a pattern across a run of choices. Use them to
see whether the DJ is applying the guidance you intended, not to demand that
every pick has the same rationale.

### Why does the selection reason not sound like the link I heard?

Because they serve different audiences. The reason is private Booth Log context
for understanding a choice; the link is the DJ speaking to listeners.

### When should I change something?

When the same issue appears repeatedly in a comparable listener moment. One
unexpected selection is usually variety. A week of the same kind of unexpected
selection is a tuning clue.
